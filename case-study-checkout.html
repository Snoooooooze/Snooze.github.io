Redesigning checkout for a food delivery app

How I helped a delivery startup recover customers who were giving up at the final step of their order.

Role
Lead designer
Team
1 product manager, 2 engineers, 1 researcher
Timeline
8 weeks
Tools
Figma, Maze, Google Analytics

The problem

Analytics showed that 38% of people who reached the payment step left without ordering. The team assumed prices were the cause, but nobody had asked customers directly. My goal was to find the real reason and fix it.

Research

I ran 8 moderated usability sessions and reviewed 120 support tickets about failed orders. Three problems came up repeatedly:

  • Delivery fees only appeared on the last screen, which felt like a surprise charge.
  • Saved cards often failed silently, with no explanation.
  • The promo code field sent people hunting for codes in other tabs, and many never came back.

"I was ready to pay, and then there was a fee I hadn't seen. I just closed the app."

Participant 4, usability session

Design decisions

Show the full cost early. I moved the delivery fee and estimated total into the cart, so nothing changes at checkout.

Make payment errors specific. Instead of "Payment failed," the new message says what went wrong and offers a button to try another card.

Collapse the promo field. It now sits behind a "Have a code?" link, so it no longer invites people to leave.

Results

After two rounds of usability testing and a 4-week live test:

38% to 24%checkout abandonment
-45%payment-related support tickets
+9%completed orders

What I learned

The team's first guess (pricing) was wrong. Talking to customers early saved us from discounting something that wasn't the problem. Next time I would bring engineers to the research sessions so they hear the feedback firsthand.
